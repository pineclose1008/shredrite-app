Revenue by Month
Purpose of this chart
To show what moves revenue from month to month. Turn on Ad spend to compare it with the collections in the same month. Tap a bar for that month's figures.
Cash Flow
Purpose of this chart
To show the money that actually moved, as the bank records it. Profit spreads the cost of a machine across its working life; cash does not, because the machine was paid for on the day. This is why a profitable month can still reduce the bank balance.
Tap a month to see exactly what the money was spent on.
Profit by Month
Purpose of this chart
To show whether the business truly made money each month.
revenue − day-to-day costs − machine cost − give-back

A machine is not charged to the month it was bought. Its cost is spread across every month it works, because that is how long it earns. Deposits are never treated as a cost, since they are returned. Tap a bar for the details.
Where the Money Goes on One Collection
Purpose of this section
To separate the cost of carrying out a collection from fixed costs such as rent and advertising, and to show how much of each collection's revenue is left to cover rent, advertising and equipment. This is what determines whether profitability depends on pricing or on volume.
Customer Economics
Purpose of this section
To show what it costs to win a customer, what that customer is worth, and whether customers return. Revenue by month records what happened; this section indicates whether it is repeatable.

CAC is short for Customer Acquisition Cost: the advertising spent to win one new customer.
advertising for the month ÷ new customers that month

Revenue per customer: total paid by one customer across all their collections.
total revenue ÷ number of different customers

Repeat rate: the share of customers who have booked more than once.
customers who booked twice or more ÷ all customers

Where a customer is worth more than they cost to win, additional advertising produces additional profit. Where few customers return, that advertising has to be repeated every month to hold the same level of work.
