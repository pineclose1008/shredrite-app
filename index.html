Cash Flow
Purpose of this chart
To show the money that actually moved, the way your bank sees it. Profit spreads a machine over ten years. Cash does not: you paid for it on the day. This is why a good month can still leave you short.
Tap a month to see exactly what the money was spent on.
Profit by Month
Purpose of this chart
To show whether the business truly made money each month.
money in − running costs − wear on machines − give-back

A machine is not counted in the month you bought it. It is counted a little each month for as long as it lasts, because that is how long it earns for you. Deposits are never counted, because you get them back. Tap a bar for the details.
Where the Money Goes on One Job
Purpose of this chart
When money is tight there are only two things you can do: charge more, or spend less. They pull in opposite directions, and picking the wrong one makes things worse. This chart tells you which one is your problem.
Customer Economics
Purpose of this chart
To show whether you can simply buy more business, and whether the customers you buy stay with you.

CAC is short for Customer Acquisition Cost. In plain words: what you spend on ads to get one new customer.
ad spend for the month ÷ new customers that month

Revenue per customer: what one customer has paid you in total.
all money collected ÷ number of different customers

Repeat rate: how many of your customers ever came back.
customers who booked twice or more ÷ all customers

If a customer pays you more than they cost to get, more ads means more money. If almost nobody comes back, you have to keep paying for new ones forever.
